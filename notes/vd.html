MF – Specialized Investment Fund Distributors

V-A plus the shorting toolkit: SIF strategies, equity derivatives and interest rate derivatives in one 150-mark paper.

Mutual funds are like a shared taxi with strict rules; SEBI has now added a 'premium shuttle' called the Specialized Investment Fund (SIF), run by the same AMCs but allowed to bet against stocks, sectors and bonds (short up to 25%) with a ₹10 lakh entry ticket. Because SIFs use futures and options to go short, anyone selling them must understand those tools, not just mutual funds. This certification therefore bundles the V-A mutual fund syllabus with equity derivatives (from Series VIII) and interest rate derivatives (from Series IV). SEBI wants SIF distributors to explain a product that can lose money in more ways than a plain fund.

Investment Landscape

Why people invest, how saving differs from investing, the main asset classes, the risks they carry, behavioural biases, risk profiling and asset allocation. It is the 'financial planning 101' base for everything that follows.

Make it stickThink of Savin's own money as a team: equity is the striker (scores big, gets injured), debt is the defender (steady, rarely scores), gold is the goalkeeper (only shines in a crisis). Asset allocation is simply picking the line-up for the match you are about to play — your goal and time horizon.
  1. What's new vs V-A: nothing material — this chapter is the V-A (2026 edition) text, so a quick revision is enough; spend the saved time on derivatives.
  2. Saving = not spending; investing = deploying savings to earn a return. Money kept idle loses purchasing power to inflation.
  3. Asset classes: real (real estate, gold, commodities) and financial (equity, debt/fixed income, cash/money market); alternatives sit outside the traditional three.
  4. Equity gives ownership and growth potential with high volatility; debt gives contractual income with credit, interest-rate and reinvestment risk.
  5. Key investment risks: inflation, market/price, interest rate, credit/default, liquidity, reinvestment, concentration, currency and regulatory risk.
  6. Systematic (market) risk cannot be diversified away; unsystematic (company-specific) risk can be reduced through diversification.
  7. Behavioural biases (overconfidence, herd mentality, loss aversion, anchoring, confirmation bias, recency) distort decisions — a distributor's job is to counter them.
  8. Risk profiling looks at both risk capacity (ability: age, income, wealth, horizon) and risk appetite/tolerance (willingness).
  9. Strategic asset allocation is the long-term mix linked to goals; tactical allocation is a short-term tilt to exploit market views.
  10. Do-it-yourself suits knowledgeable investors; most investors benefit from professional help (distributor or registered investment adviser).

Concept and Role of a Mutual Fund

What a mutual fund is, how open-ended, close-ended and interval schemes differ, the SEBI scheme categories — and the star of this exam: the Specialized Investment Fund (SIF), a new product line between mutual funds and PMS.

Make it stickA mutual fund is a shared taxi with strict traffic rules. PMS is a private car with a personal driver. SIF is the new 'premium shuttle': still run by the same taxi company (the AMC), still under taxi-style rules, but allowed to take shortcuts (short positions up to 25%) — and the ticket starts at ₹10 lakh.
  1. What's new vs V-A: the SIF section is the exam's raison d'être — the ₹10 lakh minimum, the seven investment strategies, their limits and redemption frequencies. The rest (categories, interval schemes, MF Lite) is V-A material.
  2. SIF was introduced by amending the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations to bridge the portfolio-flexibility gap between mutual funds and PMS.
  3. SIF minimum investment: ₹10 lakh per investor across ALL investment strategies of the same AMC (not per strategy).
  4. Equity-oriented SIF strategies: Equity Long-Short (min 80% equity; max 25% unhedged short via derivatives; open-ended or interval), Equity Ex-Top 100 Long-Short (min 65% of NAV in stocks outside top 100 by market cap; short max 25% in non-large-caps), Sector Rotation Long-Short (min 80% equity; max 4 sectors; short at sector level, max 25%). Redemptions: daily or any lesser frequency.
  5. Debt-oriented SIF strategies (both interval): Debt Long-Short (across duration; unhedged shorts via exchange-traded debt derivatives) and Sectoral Debt Long-Short (min 2 sectors; max 75% in one sector; short max 25% of NAV). Redemptions: once a week or lesser frequency.
  6. Hybrid SIF strategies (both interval): Active Asset Allocator Long-Short (equity, debt, equity/debt/commodity derivatives, REITs, InvITs; short max 25%) and Hybrid Long-Short (min 25% each in equity and debt; short max 25% of net assets). Redemptions: twice a week or lesser frequency.
  7. Only ONE investment strategy per SIF category can be launched by an AMC (to avoid proliferation, like MF categorisation).
  8. If SIF strategies are launched as close-ended or interval structures, their units must be listed on a recognised stock exchange.
  9. Interval scheme: transaction period minimum 2 working days; interval period minimum 15 days.
  10. SEBI categorisation (2026 circular): Equity 13 sub-categories, Debt 17, Hybrid 7, Life Cycle Funds, Other 2 (Index/ETF 95%, FoF 95%).
  11. Market-cap buckets: large cap = 1st–100th, mid cap = 101st–250th, small cap = 251st onwards by full market cap.
  12. Life Cycle Funds: glide-path, tenure 5–30 years in multiples of 5, maximum 6 open for subscription per mutual fund.
  13. MF Lite: lighter regime only for passive schemes (index funds, ETFs, FoFs).

Legal Structure of Mutual Funds in India

The three-tier trust structure — sponsor, trustees, AMC — plus custodian, RTA and AMFI. Who appoints whom, who must be independent, and how much capital is required.

Make it stickPicture a temple trust: the donor (sponsor) builds it, the trust board (trustees) guards devotees' interests, and the hired manager (AMC) runs daily rituals. The temple's jewels sit in a separate locker (custodian) so nobody can quietly pawn them.
  1. What's new vs V-A: same structure; only SIF line — AMCs wanting to launch an SIF must meet additional eligibility criteria specified by SEBI.
  2. Mutual fund is constituted as a trust under the Indian Trusts Act; trust deed registered under the Indian Registration Act, 1908, executed by the sponsor in favour of trustees.
  3. Sponsor eligibility includes positive net worth in each of the preceding 5 years, net profit in each of the preceding 5 years and average annual net profit of at least ₹10 crore over 5 years.
  4. Trustee eligibility (Regulation 11): fit and proper; not a director/officer/employee of an AMC; not a trustee of any other mutual fund. Prior SEBI approval needed.
  5. At least two-thirds of trustees must be independent (not associates of sponsor/AMC).
  6. AMC board: at least 50% directors must be non-associates of the sponsor/trustees; an AMC director cannot sit on any trustee board; one person cannot be director in more than one AMC.
  7. AMC net worth: not less than ₹50 crore, maintained continuously (sponsor's responsibility).
  8. AMC can be terminated by a majority of trustees or by 75% of unitholders; change of control needs SEBI + trustee approval and a 30-day no-load exit window for unitholders.
  9. Custodian is appointed by trustees and registered under SEBI (Custodian) Regulations, 1996; if sponsor holds 50%+ voting rights, it generally cannot be the custodian.
  10. AMFI is the industry association (not a regulator); it registers distributors (ARN) and issues the code of conduct.

Legal and Regulatory Framework

Regulators (SEBI, RBI), the SEBI MF Regulations 2026, investment restrictions (including Regulation 51 for SIF), the advertisement code with SIF branding rules, investor rights and grievance redress.

Make it stickSIF branding is like a new restaurant opened by a famous hotel chain: for 5 years it can say 'brought to you by TajGroup' in small print, but its own name must be the bigger sign — and it needs its own menu (website).
  1. What's new vs V-A: SIF-specific rules — Regulation 51 investment limits and the SIF branding/advertisement norms (distinct name & logo, 5-year use of MF brand, font size rule, separate website).
  2. Investment universe (Regulation 39): securities, money market instruments, privately placed debentures, securitised debt, gold and silver instruments, and others SEBI specifies.
  3. Regulation 41 restrictions are applied with the Sixth Schedule; restrictions apply at the time of making the investment.
  4. Regulation 51: an SIF may invest in instruments permitted under Regulation 39; SEBI may specify the manner; investments remain subject to the Sixth Schedule restrictions applicable to MF schemes.
  5. SIF must have a distinct brand name and logo separate from the mutual fund; the sponsor's/MF's brand may be used in SIF documents and ads for 5 years from SEBI approval of the SIF.
  6. When referring to the MF brand, phrases like 'brought to you by' or 'offered by' are recommended; MF brand font size must be equal to or smaller than the SIF brand name.
  7. AMC must keep a separate website or dedicated web page exclusively for the SIF, and follow MF advertisement guidelines for all SIF strategies.
  8. Advertisement code (Fifth Schedule): no celebrities, no testimonials or rankings, standard warning 'Mutual Fund investments are subject to market risks, read all scheme related documents carefully.' with no changes.
  9. Investors can appoint up to 3 nominees; grievances must be redressed within 21 calendar days; escalation to SCORES, then SMART ODR for conciliation/arbitration.
  10. Caveat emptor applies — a prospective investor does not have the rights of an existing unitholder.

Scheme Related Information

The offer documents (SID, SAI, KIM), how and when they are updated, risk-o-meter, portfolio disclosure — and SIF's own versions: the Risk-Band (5 levels), bi-monthly portfolio disclosure and scenario analysis in the ISID.

Make it stickIf a mutual fund's risk-o-meter is a 6-level traffic light, the SIF Risk-Band is a separate 5-step ladder for a road that is already steeper — so investors never confuse the two.
  1. What's new vs V-A: SIF disclosure regime — Risk-Band with 5 levels (level 1 lowest to 5 highest), portfolio disclosure every alternate month, scenario analysis in the Investment Strategy Information Document (ISID).
  2. Mandatory documents: SID (scheme-specific), SAI (fund-level), KIM (summary that must accompany every application form).
  3. SID update: within one month from end of each half-year (data as of September and March) for open-ended and interval schemes.
  4. SAI: regular update by the end of 3 months of every financial year; material changes on an ongoing basis. KIM: at least once a half-year, within one month of half-year end.
  5. Change in fundamental attributes (Reg 22(9)(c)): addendum, public notice and an exit option of not less than 30 days without load.
  6. MF risk-o-meter: 6 levels (Low, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderately High, High, Very High), evaluated monthly.
  7. SIF Risk-Band: 5 levels; assigned at NFO; evaluated monthly; disclosed on website within 10 days of month-end; annual (31 March) disclosure with number of changes, on AMC and AMFI websites.
  8. MF portfolio disclosure: monthly/half-yearly within 10 days; debt schemes fortnightly within 5 days.
  9. SIF portfolio disclosure: as on the last day of every alternate month — May, July, September, November, January, March — within 10 days, with ISIN, on AMC and AMFI websites.
  10. Unaudited half-yearly financial results hosted within one month of half-year end (31 March and 30 September).

Fund Distribution and Channel Management Practices

Who can distribute, how they qualify (NISM, ARN, EUIN, KYD), how they are paid (full trail model), commission disclosure and due diligence — plus the rule that MF distributors can also offer SIF if they meet extra criteria.

Make it stickA distributor's income is like rent from a building he helped fill: every month he gets a slice of the current value (trail), and if the tenant (investor) switches agents, nobody collects rent for a year.
  1. What's new vs V-A: an entity distributing MFs may also offer SIF products, subject to additional SEBI eligibility criteria (this V-D certification is the qualification route). The commission rules are the same 2026 rules as V-A.
  2. Prerequisites: NISM certification, KYD (PAN, address proof, biometrics at CAMS PoS), ARN from AMFI (minimum age 18), empanelment with AMCs. Employees of corporate distributors need an EUIN.
  3. ARN and EUIN applications only online via AMFI website from 1 October 2024.
  4. As per SEBI Master Circular of 20 March 2026: full trail model in all schemes — no upfront commission or upfronting of trail (directly or indirectly, cash or kind).
  5. Trail commission = AUM × annual rate ÷ 365, computed on daily balances; no commission on the distributor's own investments.
  6. Additional commission for new investors (new PAN) from B-30 cities and new women investors: 1% of first lump sum (min 1-year holding) or 1% of first-year SIP, capped at ₹2,000; paid from the 2 bps investor-education set-aside.
  7. Transaction charges framework (for subscriptions above ₹10,000) withdrawn by SEBI in August 2025.
  8. Commission disclosure / due diligence applies to distributors with >20 locations, or >₹100 crore non-institutional AUM, or >₹1 crore commission p.a. industry-wide, or >₹50 lakh from a single AMC.
  9. Change of distributor: no trail to either distributor for 12 months.
  10. Nominee/legal heir of a deceased MFD receives trail on existing assets; no new business under that ARN; investors get 15 days to object to ARN change.

Net Asset Value, Total Expense Ratio and Pricing of Units

How NAV is computed, fair valuation, the new 2026 base-expense-ratio TER slabs, loads and IDCW. Number-heavy but formula-light.

Make it stickNAV is the price tag on one slice of a pizza: (whole pizza value − what you still owe the delivery guy) ÷ number of slices.
  1. What's new vs V-A: identical to the V-A 2026 edition — base expense ratio slabs (2.10% / 1.85% on first ₹500 crore), brokerage cap 0.06% cash / 0.02% derivatives, close-ended 1.00% / 0.80%. Re-check if your V-A notes had the older 2.25% slabs.
  2. NAV = (Market value of investments + receivables + accrued income + other assets − liabilities − accrued expenses) ÷ units outstanding.
  3. Equity-oriented scheme for TER purposes: invests at least 65% of net assets in equity (other than index fund/ETF).
  4. Open-ended TER slabs (equity / other): first ₹500 cr 2.10/1.85; next ₹250 cr 1.90/1.65; next ₹1,250 cr 1.60/1.40; next ₹3,000 cr 1.50/1.25; next ₹5,000 cr 1.40/1.15; next ₹40,000 cr reduce 0.05% per ₹5,000 cr; balance 0.95/0.70.
  5. Index fund/ETF TER cap 0.90%; FoF caps: 0.90% (investing in liquid/index/ETF), 2.10% (≥65% in equity schemes), 1.85% (others).
  6. Close-ended & interval: equity-oriented 1.00%, others 0.80%.
  7. Over and above the base: brokerage/transaction cost up to 0.06% (cash) and 0.02% (derivatives) of trade value, plus statutory levies (GST).
  8. Entry load is banned; exit load (net of GST) is credited back to the scheme.
  9. IDCW record date is 5 calendar days from the AMC's notice; NAV falls by the amount distributed.
  10. AT-1 bonds are valued on yield-to-call basis; TER must be disclosed daily on AMC and AMFI websites.

Chapter 8 · Weight: 3 marks

Taxation

How MF schemes and investors are taxed: equity-oriented vs others, STCG/LTCG after July 2024, ₹1.25 lakh exemption, IDCW at slab, STT, stamp duty, set-off rules and TDS.

Make it stickThe fund itself is a tax-free pipe; tax is collected only at the tap — when you redeem (capital gains) or receive IDCW.
  1. What's new vs V-A: no SIF-specific tax rules are given in the workbook; it is the V-A tax chapter. Keep the post-23 July 2024 rates handy.
  2. MF scheme income is exempt under Section 10(23D); investors pay tax on capital gains and IDCW.
  3. Equity-oriented fund: more than 65% in listed domestic equity. FoFs investing in equity funds are non-equity for tax.
  4. Holding periods: 12 months for listed securities and equity-oriented funds; 24 months for others.
  5. Equity-oriented: STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh per financial year (grandfathering at 31 Jan 2018 NAV).
  6. Debt-oriented funds (>65% debt) and debt ETFs: gains taxed at slab rate (MSR) regardless of holding period.
  7. Hybrid 35–65% equity, FoFs/international: STCG at slab, LTCG 12.5% after 24 months; Equity & Gold ETFs: LTCG 12.5% after 12 months.
  8. IDCW is taxed at slab in investor's hands; TDS 10% on IDCW above ₹10,000.
  9. Stamp duty 0.005% on issue of units (purchases, SIP, STP, reinvestment); 0.015% on transfer.
  10. STT 0.001% on redemption of equity-oriented units (seller); not on purchases or debt funds.
  11. Capital losses can't be set off against other heads; LTCL only against LTCG; carry forward 8 years. Bonus stripping: buy within 3 months before record date, sell within 9 months after.

Investor Services

NFOs, allotment, account statements, who can invest, KYC, financial transactions, cut-off times, systematic plans, non-financial changes and TATs — plus SIF tweaks: different subscription vs redemption frequency and SIP/SWP/STP within the ₹10 lakh threshold.

Chapter 10 · Weight: 5 marks

Risk, Return and Performance of Funds

Risk factors in equity, debt, hybrid and international schemes, drivers of return, measures of return (simple, annualised, CAGR, XIRR, rolling) and risk (standard deviation, beta, duration), and credit-risk safeguards.

Make it stickReturn measures are like describing a road trip: 'we covered 300 km' (simple), 'that's 50 km/h' (annualised), and 'our average compounded speed with stops' (CAGR).
  1. What's new vs V-A: no SIF-specific content; but the duration, beta and standard-deviation ideas reappear heavily in the derivatives modules — learn them once, use them thrice.
  2. Simple return = (Later value − Initial value) ÷ Initial value; Annualised = Simple × 12 ÷ months.
  3. Compounded / CAGR = (Later value ÷ Initial value)^(1/n) − 1; CAGR assumes IDCW reinvested.
  4. Holding-period returns: CAGR above 1 year, absolute below 1 year. Rolling returns average many consecutive holding periods.
  5. Investor return differs from scheme return due to loads, taxes and timing.
  6. Standard deviation measures total volatility; beta measures sensitivity to the market (market beta = 1).
  7. Debt fund risk drivers: interest-rate risk (measured by modified duration), credit risk, liquidity risk; longer duration = higher rate sensitivity.
  8. Equity risk drivers: market, sector concentration, stock-specific and liquidity risks.
  9. International funds add currency and country risk.

Chapter 11 · Weight: 5 marks

Mutual Fund Scheme Performance

Benchmarks (PRI vs TRI, tiered benchmarks), risk-adjusted measures (Sharpe, Treynor, Alpha, Information Ratio), tracking error and performance disclosure rules.

Make it stickA benchmark is the par score on a golf course: shooting 72 means nothing until you know par is 70 or 75.
  1. What's new vs V-A: no SIF-specific content; same benchmarking chapter. Note how tracking error and beta connect directly to index futures hedging in Chapter 15.
  2. Mutual funds must benchmark against the Total Return Index (TRI), which includes dividends; a Price Return Index ignores dividends.
  3. Sharpe ratio = (Rs − Rf) ÷ Standard deviation — return per unit of total risk.
  4. Treynor ratio = (Rs − Rf) ÷ Beta — return per unit of market risk; best for diversified equity schemes.
  5. Alpha = actual return − optimal (beta-implied) return; positive alpha suggests out-performance.
  6. Information Ratio = (Portfolio return − Benchmark return) ÷ SD of excess returns; the only risk-adjusted measure mandated for daily disclosure, using tier-I benchmark and daily returns.
  7. Tracking error = standard deviation of the difference between fund and benchmark returns; lower is better for index funds.
  8. Risk-o-meters of both scheme and benchmark must be shown when performance is compared.

Basics of Derivatives

Brand-new territory (not in V-A). What a derivative is, its history in India and abroad, the four generic products, participants, OTC vs exchange-traded, uses and risks. SIF managers use exactly these tools to go short.

Make it stickA derivative is a shadow: it has no life of its own and moves only because the object (the underlying) moves. SIF short positions are built entirely from such shadows.
  1. Derivative = contract whose value is derived from an underlying (commodities, energy, metals, shares, bonds, currencies, indices).
  2. Ind AS 109 criteria: value linked to underlying; settled on a future date; no full cash outlay on trade date (this gives leverage).
  3. SEBI's L.C. Gupta Committee (24 members) set up 18 Nov 1996, report 17 Mar 1998 — recommended derivatives be declared 'securities'. J.R. Varma group (June 1998, report Oct 1998) designed risk containment/margining.
  4. SCRA amended in 1999 to include derivatives within 'securities'; Section 18A makes exchange-traded, clearing-house-settled derivatives legal and valid.
  5. India timeline: index futures June 2000 → index options June 2001 → stock options July 2001 → stock futures November 2001; MSEI derivatives Feb 2013.
  6. Global milestones: CBOT 1848 (forwards), first exchange-traded futures 1865, CBOE listed options 1973, first stock index futures by Kansas City Board of Trade 1982.
  7. Four generic products: forwards (OTC, customised), futures (exchange, standardised), options (right not obligation; OTC and exchange), swaps (series of forwards, mainly OTC).
  8. Participants: hedgers (reduce risk), speculators/traders (take risk for profit, provide liquidity), arbitrageurs (exploit price differences, keep markets aligned).
  9. OTC: customised, decentralised credit risk, little transparency; exchange-traded: standardised, clearing corporation guarantees settlement (novation).
  10. Risks: counterparty, price, liquidity, legal/regulatory and operational (fraud, poor documentation). Read the Model Risk Disclosure Document from the broker.

Understanding the Index

Brand-new territory. How stock indices are built (market-cap, free-float, price-weighted, equal-weighted), what makes a good index, impact cost, index maintenance and the 2025 SEBI norms for non-benchmark index derivatives.

Make it stickA free-float index is like weighing a crowd by how many people are actually on the dance floor, not how many bought tickets — promoter shares locked in the coat room don't count.

Introduction to Forwards and Futures

Brand-new territory and the single biggest chapter. Forwards vs futures, contract specifications (lot sizes, expiry days, settlement prices), margins and MTM, basis, open interest, cost-of-carry pricing, and the use of futures by hedgers, speculators and arbitrageurs.

Make it stickA futures contract is a forward that went to finishing school: same promise to buy later at today's price, but now standardised, guaranteed by the clearing corporation and settled every evening (MTM) so no one can run away.
  1. Forward limitations: liquidity risk and counterparty (default) risk; futures fix these via exchange trading and clearing guarantee.
  2. Index derivative contract value: not less than ₹15 lakh at introduction; lot fixed so value is ₹15–20 lakh on review day; lot a multiple of 5 (minimum 10).
  3. Stock derivative contract value: not less than ₹5 lakh; ₹5–10 lakh on review; lot a multiple of 25 (minimum 50). Lots reviewed every 6 months with 2 weeks' notice; futures and options lot sizes are the same.
  4. Futures can have maximum maturity of 12 months; exchanges usually run 3 contracts (near, next, far).
  5. SEBI (26 May 2025): each exchange's equity-derivative expiries limited to Tuesday or Thursday; one weekly benchmark index option per exchange; all other contracts monthly (last Tuesday/Thursday). NSE expiries: last Tuesday; BSE: last Thursday.
  6. Pre-open session for futures (29 May 2025): 9:00–9:15 am call auction, order entry 9:00–9:10 with random closure in the last 2 minutes; normal trading 9:15 am–3:30 pm.
  7. Daily settlement price = VWAP of last 30 minutes (theoretical price if not traded); final settlement price = closing price of underlying on expiry day.
  8. Basis = Spot − Futures. Futures > spot ⇒ negative basis (contango); spot > futures ⇒ positive basis (backwardation). Basis becomes zero at expiry.
  9. Margins: initial margin and extreme loss margin paid upfront by BOTH buyer and seller; MTM profits/losses settled daily.
  10. Open interest = contracts outstanding (one side only); volume = contracts traded in a period.
  11. Fair price (cost of carry) = Spot + Cost of carry − Income; F = S(1+r−q)^T or S·e^((r−q)T). Futures above fair value ⇒ cash-and-carry arbitrage; below ⇒ reverse cash-and-carry.
  12. Hedge contracts for a portfolio = (Portfolio value × Beta) ÷ (Futures price × Lot size). Portfolio beta = weighted average of stock betas.
  13. Long hedge protects a future purchase; short hedge protects holdings; cross hedge uses a related asset; choose a contract expiring just after the hedge end date.
  14. Calendar spread = long one expiry, short another on the same underlying; computed versus the near month; becomes a naked position when the near month expires.

Introduction to Options

Brand-new territory. Calls and puts, buyer vs writer, European vs American, contract specs, moneyness, intrinsic and time value, payoffs and breakevens, pricing factors, the Greeks, Black-Scholes, implied volatility.

Make it stickAn option is a cinema ticket bought in advance: you paid for the right to watch (premium), but you are not obliged to go. The cinema (writer) must keep the seat ready if you turn up — that's why the writer needs a deposit (margin).
  1. Call = right to buy; put = right to sell. Buyer pays premium and has rights; writer receives premium and has obligations.
  2. All exchange-traded index and stock options in India are European (exercise only on expiry); options are cash-settled at the closing price of the underlying.
  3. Moneyness — call ITM when spot > strike; put ITM when spot < strike; ATM when strike ≈ spot; OTM otherwise.
  4. Premium = intrinsic value + time value. Call IV = max(S − X, 0); put IV = max(X − S, 0). ATM/OTM options are all time value.
  5. Breakeven: call = strike + premium; put = strike − premium (same BEP for long and short).
  6. Long option: loss limited to premium, profit unlimited (put profit capped at strike − premium); short option: profit limited to premium, loss potentially unlimited. Option buyers pay no margin; writers do.
  7. Pricing factors: spot ↑ → call ↑ put ↓; strike ↑ → call ↓ put ↑; volatility ↑ → both ↑; longer time → both ↑; interest rate ↑ → call ↑ put ↓.
  8. Greeks: Delta (price sensitivity, 'hedge ratio'; call 0 to +1, put 0 to −1), Gamma (change in delta, positive for long options), Theta (time decay, usually negative for long options), Vega (volatility sensitivity, positive for long options), Rho (interest sensitivity; call +, put −).
  9. Index options on Nifty 50 and Sensex can run up to 5 years: weekly, monthly, 3 quarterly and 8 half-yearly expiries. Sensex options: lot 20, 101 strikes (50 ITM, 1 ATM, 50 OTM), strike interval 100 points.
  10. Black-Scholes (1973) for European options; binomial model by William Sharpe (1978) — iterative. Five inputs: spot, strike, volatility, time, risk-free rate.
  11. Implied volatility is backed out of the option price; high IV ⇒ traders tend to sell options; low IV ⇒ buy.

Strategies using Equity Futures and Equity Options

Brand-new territory. Hedging with stock and index futures, speculation with leverage, cash-and-carry and calendar spreads, and option strategies: vertical spreads, straddles, strangles, covered call, collar, butterfly, protective put, put-call parity, delta hedging, OI/PCR/rollover/VIX.

Make it stickOption strategies are Lego: a covered call is 'long stock + short call' and behaves like a short put; a protective put is 'long stock + long put' and behaves like a long call. Learn the bricks and every structure becomes obvious.
  1. Hedging a planned purchase: long futures locks the price; a planned sale: short futures. Futures leverage magnifies ROI (e.g., 17.69% on margin vs 3.62% outright in the workbook).
  2. Cash-and-carry: buy spot + sell overpriced futures; reverse: sell spot + buy underpriced futures. Profit is locked whatever the expiry price.
  3. Bull call spread: buy lower-strike call, sell higher-strike call (net debit); max profit = strike gap − net premium; BEP = lower strike + net debit.
  4. Bull put spread: sell higher-strike put, buy lower-strike put (net credit); BEP = higher strike − net credit. Bear call spread is a net credit; bear put spread a net debit.
  5. Horizontal (calendar) spread: same strike, different expiries; diagonal: different strikes and expiries — no simple payoff chart.
  6. Long straddle: buy call + put, same strike & expiry; bets on big move/volatility; BEPs = strike ± total premium; max loss = total premium.
  7. Long strangle: buy OTM call + OTM put (different strikes) — cheaper than straddle; short straddle/strangle profit from stable prices with unlimited risk.
  8. Covered call (long stock + short call) = synthetic short put; collar adds a long OTM put to floor the downside; protective put (long stock + long put) = synthetic long call.
  9. Long call butterfly: buy 1 ITM, sell 2 ATM, buy 1 OTM call — limited profit and loss, expects low volatility.
  10. Put-call parity (European only): C + X·e^(−rt) = P + S0. Synthetic long futures = long ATM call + short ATM put.
  11. Delta hedging: futures delta ≈ 1; keep the combined delta at zero and rebalance as delta changes.
  12. Futures-equivalent OI (delta-adjusted) introduced by SEBI. Price ↑ + OI ↑ = long build-up (bullish); price ↑ + OI ↓ = short covering; price ↓ + OI ↑ = short build-up (bearish); price ↓ + OI ↓ = long unwinding.
  13. PCR = put OI (or volume) ÷ call OI; treated as contrarian — very high PCR may signal oversold, very low may signal overbought. India VIX (NSE, from Nifty options) shows expected 30-day volatility and is negatively correlated with Nifty.
  14. Roll cost = (Next series price − Current series price) ÷ Current series price.

Introduction to Interest Rate, Interest Rate Instruments and Fixed Income Market

Brand-new depth (V-A only touched debt basics). Interest rate concepts, bond types, yield curve shapes, day-count conventions, accrued interest, yield measures, price-yield behaviour, duration, PV01, convexity, and India's G-sec, corporate bond and money markets.

Make it stickA bond price and its yield sit on opposite ends of a see-saw. Duration tells you how long the see-saw plank is — the longer the plank, the bigger the swing for the same push.
  1. Effective rate = (1 + r/n)^n − 1 (6% compounded half-yearly = 6.09%). Real rate ≈ nominal − inflation; exact: (1+r)(1+i) = (1+R).
  2. Bond types: G-secs/SDLs, municipal, corporate, securitised; zero-coupon (T-bills, CMBs, STRIPS), floating rate, inflation-indexed, callable/puttable, perpetual, AT1/Tier 2, masala bonds.
  3. Yield curve shapes: normal (upward), inverted (short > long, may signal slowdown), flat, humped. Shifts: parallel, steepening (spread widens), flattening (spread narrows).
  4. Day count (per workbook): Actual/Actual for corporate bonds; 30/360 for G-secs; Actual/365 for money market; Actual/360 for swaps. Count first day, exclude last.
  5. Dirty (invoice) price = clean price + accrued interest; trades quoted clean, settled dirty.
  6. Current yield = coupon ÷ clean price. YTM is the IRR assuming reinvestment at YTM. T-bill BEY = (FV − P)/P × 365/days; discount yield = (FV − P)/FV × 360/days (used for 91-day T-bill futures).

  8. Macaulay duration = PV-weighted average time to cash flows; equals maturity for a zero-coupon bond; falls with higher coupon or higher yield; rises with maturity.
  9. Modified duration = Macaulay ÷ (1 + y/n); % price change ≈ −MD × Δy. PV01 = Dirty price × MD ÷ 10,000. Portfolio duration = weighted average.
  10. Convexity corrects duration for large yield moves; positive convexity means price rises more than it falls for the same yield change.
  11. G-sec auctions by RBI: minimum bid ₹10,000; uniform or multiple price; non-competitive bidding for retail at weighted average price; NDS-OM (Aug 2005, run by CCIL's Clearcorp); OTC trades reported within 15 minutes; RBI Retail Direct.

Brand-new territory, but light. Definition and economic role of derivatives with interest rates as the underlying: FRAs, bond forwards, IRFs, interest rate options (caps, floors, collars), swaps, swaptions; participants; OTC vs exchange.

Strategies using Exchange Traded Interest Rate Derivatives

Brand-new territory. How hedgers, speculators and arbitrageurs use IRFs and IROs: short and long hedges, duration-based portfolio hedging, hedging future borrowing, view-based trades, cash-and-carry with bonds, synthetic T-bills, calendar and inter-bond spreads, and limitations.

Make it stickA bond fund manager fearing a rate hike is like a farmer fearing a price crash before harvest: he sells futures today. If rates rise, bond prices fall, but the short futures gain fills the hole.
  1. Choose instrument by tenor (3-month rate → T-bill futures; 10-year rate → GOI bond futures/options), side by rate view, and contract month by timing of the expected change.
  2. Expect rates to RISE: sell GOI bond futures, buy GOI bond puts, sell T-bill futures, BUY overnight MIBOR futures. Expect rates to FALL: the opposite.
  3. Short hedge protects bonds already held (sell futures); long hedge locks yield on future inflows, e.g., an insurer expecting premiums (buy futures).
  4. Duration-based hedge: number of lots = (Portfolio MD × Portfolio value) ÷ (Futures MD × Futures price × 2,000). E.g., ₹26 crore, MD 6.1, futures MD 4.7, price 98.50 ⇒ ≈ 1,713 lots.
  5. A corporate planning to issue bonds can sell IRFs to hedge a rise in rates (basis risk remains since instruments differ).
  6. Futures give leverage: no accrued interest earned on a long futures position.
  7. Buying bond futures increases portfolio duration; selling decreases it — a quick, cheap way to adjust duration.
  8. Regular arbitrage: buy bond (funded in repo) + sell overpriced futures; reverse arbitrage: sell bond + buy underpriced futures + lend in repo.
  9. Synthetic T-bill: long a long-dated G-sec + short its 3-month futures = locked 3-month return.
  10. Calendar spread (same bond, different months) attracts lower margin; inter-bond spread (different bonds, same expiry) bets on curve steepening/flattening.

📖 Glossary

Specialized Investment Fund (SIF)New product line under the MF Regulations, between MFs and PMS, allowing long-short strategies; minimum ₹10 lakh.
ISIDInvestment Strategy Information Document — the SIF offer document; must include scenario analysis of expected loss.
Risk-BandFive-level pictorial risk meter used for SIF strategies (instead of the six-level risk-o-meter).
Unhedged short exposureA net short position through derivatives not offset by a long holding; capped at 25% of NAV in SIF.
Interval schemeScheme open for transactions only during transaction periods (min 2 working days) separated by interval periods (min 15 days).
UnderlyingThe asset or rate from which a derivative derives its value.
ForwardCustomised OTC agreement to buy/sell at a future date at a price fixed today.
FuturesStandardised exchange-traded forward with daily MTM and clearing guarantee.
Option premiumPrice the option buyer pays the writer for the right.
Strike pricePrice at which the option holder may buy (call) or sell (put).
BasisSpot price − futures price; zero at expiry.
ContangoFutures above spot (negative basis); market expects prices to rise.
BackwardationFutures below spot (positive basis).
Cost of carryFinancing (and storage) cost minus income from holding the underlying until delivery.
Mark to market (MTM)Daily settlement of futures profits and losses at the daily settlement price.
Open interestNumber of contracts outstanding (counted on one side).
NovationClearing corporation becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer.
Impact costPercentage price degradation versus the ideal price when executing an order of a given size.
Free floatShares readily available for trading, excluding promoter and locked-in holdings.
DeltaChange in option premium for a unit change in the underlying; the hedge ratio.
ThetaTime decay of an option's premium.
VegaSensitivity of option premium to volatility.
Implied volatilityVolatility backed out from the market price of an option.
Covered callLong stock + short call; behaves like a short put.
Protective putLong stock + long put; behaves like a long call.
StraddleCall and put with the same strike and expiry.
StrangleOTM call and OTM put with different strikes, same expiry.
Put-call ratioPut OI or volume divided by call OI or volume; a sentiment (contrarian) indicator.
Clean / dirty priceBond price without / with accrued interest.
Modified durationApproximate % price change of a bond for a 1% change in yield.
PV01Rupee change in bond price for a 1 bp change in yield.
FRAForward Rate Agreement — OTC contract fixing a borrowing/lending rate for a future period, cash-settled.
Overnight MIBORFBIL benchmark from the first hour of NDS-CALL trades; underlying for OIS and MIBOR futures.
Black (1976) modelOption model using futures/forward prices; used for exchange-traded IROs in India.
Bermudan optionExercisable on specified dates before expiry.
