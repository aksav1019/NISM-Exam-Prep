AIF Category III Distributors

Hedge funds, Indian style: learn the 2x leverage leash, the 10% concentration cap and the fee maths before you sell a single unit.

A Category III AIF is India's legal home for hedge-fund style money: a private pool where rich investors and institutions hand money to a manager who can go long, go short, use futures and options and borrow (up to 2 times NAV). It is like a high-performance race car: it can go much faster than a family sedan (a mutual fund), but only licensed drivers with deep pockets should get in. The distributor is the showroom salesperson who must check the buyer can handle it and explain every risk and fee before the keys change hands. NISM-Series-XIX-B tests that you understand the car, the rulebook (SEBI AIF Regulations, 2012), the fee meter and the tax bill well enough to sell it honestly.

Chapter 1 · Weight: 4%

Overview of Alternative Investments

Introduces alternative investments as everything outside traditional stocks, bonds, cash, mutual funds and ETFs. Their defining trait is illiquidity, and they exist to improve risk-adjusted returns for sophisticated investors through diversification.

Make it stickThink of a traditional portfolio as a supermarket trolley: everything has a barcode and can be returned at the counter (liquid). Alternatives are like buying a custom-built boat: potentially more fun and value, but you cannot return it tomorrow.
  1. Alternative investment = financial asset outside conventional categories (stocks, bonds, cash); essential characteristic is illiquidity
  2. Traditional investments are on-market and liquid: listed shares and bonds, open-ended MFs, ETFs, simple exchange-traded derivatives
  3. Dual category: illiquid underlying assets but liquid instruments, such as REITs, InvITs, security receipts, close-ended debt funds
  4. SEBI (AIF) Regulations, 2012 define an AIF as a privately pooled investment vehicle, funds pooled from India or abroad for a defined investment policy
  5. Excluded from AIF definition: mutual funds, collective investment schemes, family trusts, ESOP/employee benefit trusts, holding companies, securitisation SPVs, funds under RBI or other regulators
  6. Hedge funds invest across asset classes, use complex strategies, go long and short and use significant leverage at fund level
  7. SEBI definition of hedge fund: AIF that employs diverse or complex trading strategies and invests in securities with diverse risks or complex products including listed and unlisted derivatives
  8. DPIIT start-up: not more than 10 years old and turnover not above Rs 100 crore in any financial year
  9. PE buyouts typically target 51% or more of share capital or voting rights; LBOs use significant leverage
  10. Benefits of alternatives: diversification, alpha from off-market strategies, growth capital; limitations: complexity, low transparency, illiquidity, no regular income
  11. AIFs must give Persons with Disabilities full cooperation, accessibility and equal opportunity

Chapter 2 · Weight: 4%

Growth of Alternative Investment Funds in India and Suitability of Category III AIFs

Traces hedge funds from A.W. Jones (1949) to the SEBI AIF Regulations of May 21, 2012, describes all AIF types and the three categories, and explains why Category III is the riskiest and suits only institutions and ultra-HNIs.

Make it stickBefore 2012 Indian rich investors had to send money abroad under LRS to touch a hedge fund. The 2012 regulations built a domestic 'Category III' garage, and GIFT City later added a tax-neutral offshore wing inside India.
  1. First hedge fund: Alfred Winslow Jones, A.W. Jones & Co., 1949 (incentive fees, short selling, leverage)
  2. SEBI (AIF) Regulations notified May 21, 2012; Category III is the home of hedge funds
  3. LRS started Feb 4, 2004 at USD 25,000; now USD 2,50,000 per person per financial year
  4. FEMA Notification 355/2015 (Nov 16, 2015): AIF investment is foreign investment only if sponsor or manager is not Indian owned and controlled
  5. Category I: start-ups, early-stage, social ventures, SMEs, infrastructure (VCF, angel, SME, social impact, infra, special situations, CDMDF)
  6. Category II: residual category; no leverage except day-to-day operational needs (PE funds, debt funds)
  7. Category III: diverse or complex trading strategies, may use leverage including listed or unlisted derivatives (hedge funds, short-term trading funds)
  8. Angel fund: at least 5 accredited investors before first close; investment per investee Rs 10 lakh to Rs 25 crore
  9. CDMDF: close-ended, trust, 15-year tenure from first close; buys investment-grade listed bonds with residual maturity up to 5 years from debt MFs during dislocation
  10. Special situation assets include stressed loans, ARC security receipts and securities of companies rated 'D' with defaults continuing 90 days
  11. Category III is the riskiest category; suited only to institutional investors and ultra-HNIs
  12. Key PPM sections for suitability: Investment Objective and Strategy, Term of Fund, Manager (prior experience), Redemption (lock-in, exit load)
  13. Market status: 2014 AIFs registered as of Aug 16, 2026 (21 in 2012); commitments above Rs 16.94 lakh crore as of Mar 31, 2026

Chapter 3 · Weight: 12%

Introduction to Category III AIF Ecosystem

The players (investors, sponsors, trustees, managers), the money flow (commitment, drawdown, dry powder), the fee vocabulary (hurdle, high-water mark, catch-up, waterfall) and how Cat III compares with PMS, mutual funds and SIFs.

Make it stickPicture a cricket franchise: investors are the owners who commit money, the sponsor founds the club and must put its own money on the line (skin in the game), the manager is the captain picking the playing XI, and the trustee is the match referee keeping everyone honest.
  1. Minimum investment Rs 1 crore per investor; Rs 25 lakh for employees or directors of the AIF or its manager (not applicable to accredited investors)
  2. Minimum corpus Rs 20 crore per scheme; maximum 1,000 investors per scheme
  3. Sponsor or manager continuing interest: lower of 5% of corpus or Rs 10 crore, in cash, not via fee waiver, locked in till full distribution
  4. Concentration: up to 10% of investable funds in one investee company (20% for Large Value Funds); listed equity limit may be computed on investable funds or NAV
  5. Large Value Fund for Accredited Investors: each investor (other than manager, sponsor, employees, directors) is an accredited investor investing at least Rs 25 crore
  6. Committed capital minus drawn-down (invested) capital = dry powder
  7. First close within 12 months of SEBI communication or eligibility to launch the scheme
  8. Management fee typically 1-2% (Chapter 6 says up to 2.5%); performance fee typically up to 20% of additional return; range 0-30%
  9. Exit load after lock-in typically 0-5% of NAV; 2-year lock-in common but not mandated by SEBI
  10. High-water mark: higher of subscription price or highest net NAV at end of any previous financial year
  11. Distribution waterfall: return of capital, then hurdle (preferred) return, then catch-up to manager, then split of excess
  12. Cat III gets no tax pass-through; fund pays tax on its income
  13. Stewardship Code for AIFs (listed equity) has principles on policy, conflicts, monitoring, intervention, voting and periodic reporting
  14. Comparisons: PMS minimum Rs 50 lakh, net worth Rs 5 crore, no pooling; MF minimum Rs 500, daily NAV, public issue; SIF minimum Rs 10 lakh; Cat III private placement, NAV daily/monthly/quarterly

Chapter 4 · Weight: 12%

Category III AIF: Fund Structures and Service Providers

Covers legal forms (trust, company, LLP, body corporate), open versus closed-ended structures, offshore, onshore, unified, master-feeder and parallel structures, and the role of each service provider from merchant banker to auditor.

Make it stickA master-feeder fund is like a big restaurant kitchen (master in India) fed by delivery counters in Mauritius and Singapore (feeders). A parallel structure is like two separate restaurants ordering from the same market but cooking independently.
  1. Legal forms: trust (registered under Registration Act, 1908), company (Companies Act, 2013), LLP (LLP Act, 2008), body corporate
  2. Trust structure: settlor settles the trust, appoints trustee via trust deed; trustee appoints manager via Investment Management Agreement (signed once for the trust, not per scheme)
  3. Open-ended: continuous subscription, variable corpus, redemptions monthly/quarterly/half-yearly; closed-ended: commitment period typically 3-5 years, usually 2-year lock-in then exit with load
  4. Unified structure: domestic and offshore money pooled in one onshore AIF; no FIPB approval, automatic route
  5. Master-feeder: offshore feeders (Mauritius, Singapore) invest in Indian master; management fee charged at master, feeder charges a token (e.g., USD 1,000)
  6. Parallel (co-investment) structure: offshore funds invest directly alongside the onshore AIF; Indian manager gives non-binding advice; usually costlier fees
  7. Merchant banker files PPM with SEBI, gives due diligence certificate, must not be an associate of AIF, sponsor, manager or trustee
  8. RTA appointment is compulsory: RTAs act as collecting agents for stamp duty on issue, transfer and sale of AIF units
  9. Custodian is compulsory for Cat III, appointed before first investment of the scheme; must be SEBI-registered; not a related party unless conditions met
  10. Fund administration is not a regulated function in India
  11. Annual PPM audit: findings to trustee/board, manager's board and SEBI within 6 months of FY end; exemptions for LVFs and AIFs that raised no money
  12. Side letters and MFN clauses give differential rights; manager usually creates a separate unit class to respect fiduciary duty
  13. Wrapper: supplement to the domestic PPM for offshore distribution in a unified structure
  14. Distributors can be individuals or institutions and can be empanelled with more than one Cat III AIF

Chapter 5 · Weight: 15%

Regulatory Framework

The heaviest regulatory chapter: registration, accredited investors, investment conditions, PPM filing (GARUDA), tenure and extensions, demat, leverage and concentration limits, CDS rules, reporting, winding-up, CTR, FATCA/CRS, insider trading, PMLA, FEMA and ICDR lock-ins.

Make it stickSEBI's rulebook works like a gym trainer for a weight-lifter: lift heavy (leverage) but never more than 2x your body weight (NAV); if you overload, tell the coach (custodian) and everyone in the gym (investors) before 10 AM next day and put the extra weight down by close of the next working day.
  1. Registration: Form A, application fee Rs 1 lakh, registration fee Rs 15 lakh; certificate valid till the AIF is wound up; refusal communicated within 30 days
  2. In-principle approval: register trust or LLP deed within 6 months; may accept commitments but not call money before registration
  3. Key investment team: at least one person with NISM XIX-C or XIX-E, and one with a professional qualification (can be the same person)
  4. Accredited investor (individual/HUF): income at least Rs 2 crore, or net worth Rs 7.5 crore (Rs 3.75 crore financial), or income Rs 1 crore plus net worth Rs 5 crore (Rs 2.5 crore financial); body corporate or trust: net worth Rs 50 crore
  5. Accreditation valid 2 years (3 years if criteria met in each of preceding 2 FYs); agency is subsidiary of an exchange or depository
  6. Change of investment strategy needs two-thirds of investors by value; dealing with associates or 50%+ investor needs 75% approval
  7. Joint investors: max 2 persons (spouse, parent or child); open-ended scheme corpus below Rs 20 crore must be restored within 3 months, intimate SEBI within 2 days
  8. PPM via merchant banker at least 10 working days before launch; new schemes launch after 10 working days; AI-only funds and LVFs launch immediately on filing; PPM changes intimated within 1 month of FY end
  9. Close-ended tenure from first close; extension up to 2 years (two 1-year extensions) with two-thirds approval; AI-only funds up to 5 years; liquidation period 1 year
  10. Leverage = total exposure (long + short after permitted offsetting) / NAV, maximum 2 times; NAV excludes borrowed funds; breach: report to investors before 10 AM next working day, square off by end of next working day
  11. CDS: unhedged gross positions up to 25% of investable funds (beyond that only after informing unit holders); CDS exposure reported to custodian by next working day
  12. Overseas investment: up to 25% of investible funds, industry cap USD 1,500 million, SEBI approval valid 6 months, utilisation reported within 5 working days
  13. Corporate bonds: at least 10% of monthly secondary trades via RFQ platform; direct plan mandatory; distribution fee only on equal-trail basis, paid out of management fee
  14. Grievances redressed within 21 calendar days; compliance officer (not the CEO) reports non-compliance within 7 working days
  15. Winding-up when tenure ends, trustee decides, 75% investors by value resolve, or SEBI directs; in-specie distribution and dissolution period need 75% approval; no new liquidation schemes after Apr 25, 2024
  16. PMLA: cash transactions above Rs 10 lakh, cross-border wires above Rs 5 lakh; UBO threshold 25% (company/trust), 15% (partnership); FIU can attach property up to 180 days
  17. ICDR: preferential allotment lock-in 6 months (1 year if proceeds mainly for capex); takeover disclosure at 5% within 2 working days

Chapter 6 · Weight: 15%

Fees Structure, Fund Performance and Benchmarking

The numerical heart of the exam: management fee on gross NAV plus 18% GST, hurdle rate and high-water mark, catch-up, gross vs net returns, IRR, DPI/RVPI/TVPI, standard deviation, skewness, kurtosis, Sharpe, Treynor, maximum drawdown, VaR, benchmarks and CAPM alpha.

Make it stickA fee structure is a toll road. The management fee is the fixed toll you pay even if traffic is jammed. The incentive fee is a bonus toll charged only when you reach the destination faster than the hurdle speed AND faster than your previous best time (high-water mark).
  1. Management fee: 1% to 2.5% of Gross NAV (not committed capital), plus GST 18%; accrues from first close; paid even if fund loses money
  2. Incentive fee: usually 0-20% of return above the higher of reference hurdle and high-water mark; for closed-ended funds paid at end of tenure
  3. Set-up costs up to 1.5-2% of commitments, amortised over 36 months or fund life from first closing; operating expenses usually capped around 0.5% of AUM
  4. Indian hurdle rates typically 10-12% p.a. versus 7-8% internationally
  5. Reference hurdle compounds: previous reference hurdle x (1 + hurdle rate)
  6. Catch-up: after capital plus hurdle, manager receives catch-up share (often 100%) until it gets its pre-agreed share of total profits
  7. Mutual funds cannot charge incentive fees; PMS exit load max 3% / 2% / 1% in years 1/2/3, operating expenses max 0.5% p.a.; PMS fee may reach 2-3% plus up to 40% incentive
  8. Gross IRR uses fund-level flows before fees and taxes; Net IRR uses investor-level flows and is lower
  9. PIC = paid-in / committed; DPI = distributions / paid-in; RVPI = residual value / paid-in; TVPI = DPI + RVPI; at full exit DPI = TVPI
  10. Normal distribution: mean +/- 1 sigma covers 68%, +/- 2 sigma 95%; kurtosis of normal = 3; leptokurtic above 3 (riskier)
  11. Sharpe = (Rp - Rf)/sigma, uses total risk; Treynor = (Rp - Rf)/beta, uses systematic risk; for undiversified portfolios prefer Sharpe
  12. Maximum drawdown = (trough - peak)/peak; VaR = maximum expected loss over a period at a given confidence
  13. Benchmarking agencies notified by association with at least 33% of AIFs; schemes 1 year past first close report data half-yearly (Sept 30, Mar 31); March data audited
  14. CAPM: E(R) = Rf + beta x (Rm - Rf); alpha = actual return - E(R)
  15. GIPS: present at least 5 years (or since inception), building to 10 years

Chapter 7 · Weight: 8%

Category III AIF Investment Strategies and Due Diligence Process

Explains long-only, long-short (130/30), market-neutral, directional and short-bias, global macro, convertible arbitrage, activist, merger arbitrage and pre-IPO strategies, plus the 18-point investor due diligence checklist and key legal documents.

Make it stickA long-short manager is a fruit trader who buys mangoes he thinks are under-priced and simultaneously sells apples he thinks are over-priced: he can win even if the whole fruit market falls, as long as mangoes beat apples.
  1. Long-only: buy positions only, focus on absolute return and capital preservation; may hedge with index puts or short futures
  2. Long-short: long under-priced, short over-priced; 130/30 means 130% long, 30% short, net 100% of investable funds
  3. Market-neutral: equal long and short exposure, portfolio beta zero or close to zero; still carries unsystematic (stock-specific) risk
  4. Directional: net long or net short bet on market direction; short-bias keeps a net short exposure; dedicated-long takes only long positions
  5. Global macro: long and short across currencies, bonds, equities, commodities, rates, driven by macro trends
  6. Convertible arbitrage: long convertible bond, short the same company's equity; profits from mispricing via conversion ratio
  7. Merger arbitrage: long target, short (or long) acquirer to capture the spread to the offer price; risk of deal failure
  8. Activist strategy: big stake to influence management; concentration limit 10% (20% for LVF) makes LVFs most suitable
  9. Pre-IPO: buy before IPO at a discount; subject to ICDR lock-in (6 months)
  10. LTCM (1998): leverage about 30:1 (USD 5 bn equity, USD 125 bn borrowed), collapse after Russian default; bailout USD 3.65 bn
  11. Due diligence checks: strategy, team, manager selection, track record (5 years), disciplinary history (5 years), key-person risk, alignment, fees, leverage, controls, BCP, ESG, transparency
  12. Key documents: subscription/contribution agreement, advisory agreement, indenture of trust (determinate or not), PPM plus wrapper, side letters

Chapter 8 · Weight: 12%

Valuation

How a Category III AIF computes NAV: frequency, classes and series of units, valuation rules by asset type, illiquidity discounts, derivative exposure and margins, mark-to-market, money market valuation, series NAV allocation and independent valuer eligibility.

Make it stickNAV is the fund's daily weighing scale. Listed shares jump on the scale at closing price, unlisted shares need an independent valuer to estimate their weight, and a thinly traded SME stock gets weighed a little lighter because you cannot sell it all in one go.
  1. NAV calculation must be independent of fund management; disclosed quarterly for close-ended and monthly for open-ended Cat III AIFs
  2. NAV per unit = (assets - liabilities) / units, rounded to four decimals, computed separately for each class or series
  3. Listed securities: closing price on valuation day; unlisted equity: initially cost, then fair value by independent valuer
  4. Unlisted non-equity with no market: amortised cost plus accrued interest; MF units: last published NAV
  5. Illiquid holdings: estimate days to exit (holding / daily volume), apply price-band fall and discount proceeds to valuation day
  6. Futures exposure = contracts x lot size x futures price; options exposure on day one = premium; Black-Scholes and MTM thereafter
  7. Margins with brokers are assets; MTM gains increase margin balance, losses increase liability
  8. Leverage = total exposure after permitted offsetting (same underlying, same maturity) / NAV, max 2
  9. Money market: overnight 1 day, notice 2-14 days, term 15 days to 1 year; valued using FBIL, CCIL, FIMMDA prices
  10. T-bills and G-Secs valued at VWAY of trades in last one hour; other debt at VWAY of all trades in the day
  11. Series NAV: series assets = total assets x opening series NAV / total opening series NAVs; series-specific fees charged to that series only
  12. Independent valuer: not an associate, at least 3 years' experience valuing unlisted securities, IBBI-registered with ICAI/ICSI/ICMAI/CFA membership, or a credit rating agency's holding or subsidiary company

Chapter 9 · Weight: 10%

Taxation

Why Category III AIFs lack pass-through status, how trust structures decide who pays tax, capital gains vs business income, STT, GST without input credit, TDS on distributions, taxation on transfer of units, and the generous IFSC (GIFT City) regime.

Make it stickCategory I and II AIFs are like a glass pipe: income flows straight through to investors who pay tax. Category III is a closed tank: the fund (through its trustee) pays the tax itself, so investors are not taxed again on distributions.
  1. Pass-through (Sec 224, Income Tax Act 2025) applies only to Category I and II AIFs; Category III is taxed at fund level
  2. Determinate irrevocable trust: business income taxed at MMR; capital gains and other income taxed as if in beneficiaries' hands (trustee as representative assessee)
  3. Indeterminate trust: taxed at MMR on entire income; revocable transfer: income taxed in hands of transferor
  4. Derivative (F&O) gains are business income taxed at MMR
  5. Listed equity (STT paid): STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5% above Rs 1.25 lakh; unlisted equity LTCG 12.5%; listed shares long-term after 12 months, unlisted after 24 months
  6. Surcharge for individuals/AoPs: 10% (Rs 50 lakh-1 crore), 15% (Rs 1-2 crore), 25% (above Rs 2 crore); capped at 15% on equity capital gains; cess 4%
  7. CBDT: listed shares held as stock-in-trade = business income; held over 12 months and treated as capital gains once, must continue; unlisted shares = capital gains
  8. Dividend as other income: only interest up to 20% of dividend deductible; company deducts TDS 10% on dividends
  9. STT: delivery equity 0.1%; option sale 0.1% (0.125% on exercise); futures sale 0.02%; non-delivery equity 0.025%
  10. GST on management and trustee fees; no input credit since no output GST; IFSC AIFs exempt
  11. AIF TDS on distributions: 10% to residents (20% without PAN); MAT 18.5% (9% in IFSC)
  12. IFSC: 100% deduction for any 10 consecutive years out of 15; STT exempt; unit holders' income exempt
  13. Stamp duty on secondary transfer of units: 0.015%

Chapter 10 · Weight: 8%

Good Practices

What an honest distributor does: build a transparent pitch book and distribution kit, serve investors before and after commitment, protect confidential data, follow a model code of conduct, avoid pass-backs and soft-dollar conflicts, and understand insurance (E&O, D&O, key-man).

Make it stickA good distributor is like a family doctor, not a pharma rep: the prescription (fund) must suit the patient's health (risk profile), side effects (risks and fees) must be explained, and the doctor must never pick a medicine because of a commission.
  1. Pitch book: confidential, for private circulation; prepared after analysing the PPM; must not mislead
  2. Pitch book sections: sponsor and manager introduction, historical performance vs benchmarks, proposed investment strategy, key terms, key risk factors
  3. Distribution kit includes registration certificate, PPM, presentation, key disclosure statement, KYC and FATCA forms, FAQs
  4. Four pillars of AIF distributorship: Marketing, Sales, Investor Support and Relationship Management
  5. Pre-commitment services: pitch books, investor meets, explaining risks, facilitating due diligence, seeking commitment
  6. Post-investment services: coordinate capital calls and drawdowns, investor approvals, point of contact, grievances, marketing follow-on funds
  7. Material strategy changes (sectors, holding period, allocation, valuation method, leverage) must be communicated to investors
  8. Model code: no assured returns, disclose hurdle, fees, catch-up and distribution commission, suitability first, no rebates or pass-backs, no negative statements about rival funds
  9. Pass-back = distributor returning part of commission to investor; prohibited
  10. Soft-dollar arrangements create conflicts; distributors should abstain and ensure disclosure
  11. Avoid commitments from own subsidiaries, associates or entities where distributor holds more than 10%
  12. Social media: disclose SEBI-registered name and registration number; principal's details first (from May 1, 2026)
  13. Insurance: Professional Indemnity (E&O) protects the fund; D&O protects directors (not fraud); key-man covers death, incapacity, kidnap, disability of key executive; typical cover about 1-2% of AUM
Four pillarsMarketing, Sales, Investor Support, Relationship Management

🎯 Exam tip'Which is NOT a good practice' questions: giving all employees access to confidential data, assuring returns, pass-backs, undisclosed soft-dollar deals.
⚠️ TrapD&O insurance does NOT cover fraud by directors, and E&O insurance protects the fund (the buyer), not the individual investor directly.

📖 Glossary

Category III AIFAIF using diverse or complex trading strategies that may employ leverage, including through listed or unlisted derivatives.
Privately pooled investment vehicleCore of the AIF definition: money pooled from select investors, not the public.
SponsorPerson who sets up and registers the AIF; must hold continuing interest of the lower of 5% of corpus or Rs 10 crore (with manager).
Investment ManagerEntity appointed to manage investments; called General Partner abroad.
Capital commitmentTotal amount an investor agrees to contribute over the fund's life.
Drawdown / capital callManager's demand for a portion of committed capital when needed.
Dry powderCommitted capital not yet drawn down.
Investable fundsCorpus net of estimated administration and management expenses over the tenure.
Accredited InvestorInvestor certified by an accreditation agency on income or net-worth criteria; gets flexibility on minimum ticket and concessions.
Large Value Fund (LVF)AIF where each investor (other than insiders) is accredited and invests at least Rs 25 crore.
PPMPrivate Placement Memorandum, the offer document of an AIF filed with SEBI through a merchant banker.
GARUDAGreen Channel: AIF Rollout upon Document Acknowledgement, SEBI's fast-track scheme launch mechanism.
Hurdle rateMinimum (preferred) return to investors before the manager earns an incentive fee.
High-water markHighest net NAV at end of any previous year (or subscription price); prevents paying incentive twice for the same gains.
Catch-upRate at which residual profits after hurdle go to the manager until it receives its agreed share of total profits.
Distribution waterfallOrder of distributing proceeds: capital, hurdle, catch-up, then remaining profit split.
Side letter / MFN clauseSpecial terms for an investor; MFN protects against others getting better terms.
Unified structureDomestic and offshore commitments pooled into one onshore AIF, via the automatic route.
Master-feederOffshore feeder funds invest into an Indian master fund.
Parallel structureOffshore funds invest directly in Indian companies alongside the onshore AIF.
WrapperSupplement attached to a domestic PPM for offshore private placement.
LeverageTotal exposure (long + short after permitted offsetting) divided by NAV; capped at 2.
Credit Default SwapCredit derivative where the protection buyer pays premium and the seller pays on a credit event of the reference entity.
Market-neutralStrategy with equal long and short exposure aiming at zero portfolio beta.
130/30Long-short fund with 130% long and 30% short positions, 100% net exposure.
Convertible arbitrageLong convertible bond and short same company's stock to exploit mispricing.
Merger arbitrageLong target, short or long acquirer to capture deal spread.
DPI / RVPI / TVPIDistributions, residual value and total value per rupee of paid-in capital.
Sharpe ratioExcess return per unit of total risk (standard deviation).
Treynor ratioExcess return per unit of systematic risk (beta).
Maximum drawdownLargest peak-to-trough fall in value in a period.
LeptokurticDistribution with kurtosis above 3 (fat tails, more outliers, riskier).
Series NAVNAV of a specific series of units after series-specific assets, liabilities and expenses.
Determinate trustTrust whose beneficiaries and shares are ascertainable from the trust deed; enables pass-through of non-business income.
Pass-backProhibited practice of a distributor sharing commission with the investor.

⚡ Quick revision: the night before

  1. Exam: 80 MCQs + 5 cases x 4 questions, 100 marks, 120 minutes, pass 60, negative 10%.
  2. AIF = privately pooled investment vehicle; MFs, family trusts, ESOP trusts and securitisation SPVs are excluded.
  3. SEBI AIF Regulations: May 21, 2012. First hedge fund: A.W. Jones, 1949.
  4. Category III = complex strategies + leverage; Category II = no leverage except operational needs.
  5. Minimum ticket Rs 1 crore (Rs 25 lakh for employees/directors); accredited investors exempt.
  6. Corpus at least Rs 20 crore per scheme; at most 1,000 investors.
  7. Skin in the game: lower of 5% of corpus or Rs 10 crore, in cash, not fee waiver.
  8. Leverage max 2 x NAV; NAV excludes borrowed funds; computed at scheme level.
  9. Leverage breach: inform investors before 10 AM next working day; square off by end of next working day; custodian tells SEBI.
  10. One investee: 10% of investable funds (20% for LVF); cost basis, not market value.
  11. Commodity derivatives: 10% of investable funds per commodity; unhedged CDS: 25% of investable funds.
  12. Overseas: 25% of investible funds; USD 1,500 mn across all AIFs; approval lapses in 6 months.
  13. Strategy change and tenure extension: two-thirds by value. Associates, winding-up, in-specie, dissolution: 75%.
  14. PPM through merchant banker 10 working days before launch; AI-only funds and LVFs launch immediately on filing.
  15. First close within 12 months; tenure counted from first close; extension up to 2 years; liquidation period 1 year.
  16. NAV: quarterly for close-ended, monthly for open-ended; computed independently of fund management.
  17. Valuation deviation >20% between two valuations or >33% in a year must be explained to investors.
  18. Quarterly investor report within 60 days; comprehensive annual activity report to SEBI within 30 calendar days of March end.
  19. Grievances: 21 calendar days. Compliance officer cannot be CEO; reports breach within 7 working days.
  20. Direct plan compulsory; distribution fee only as equal trail and only out of management fee.
  21. Management fee on gross NAV (1-2.5%) plus 18% GST; incentive fee on NAV above max(hurdle, HWM).
  22. Indian hurdle rates 10-12% vs 7-8% globally; managers like high catch-up rates.
  23. TVPI = DPI + RVPI; at full exit DPI = TVPI and RVPI = 0.
  24. Sharpe uses sigma (total risk); Treynor uses beta (systematic risk).
  25. Kurtosis above 3 = leptokurtic = more outliers; positive skew preferred.
  26. CAPM: E(R) = Rf + beta(Rm - Rf); alpha = actual - E(R).
  27. 130/30 = 130% long, 30% short, net 100%. Market-neutral beta near zero.
  28. Convertible arbitrage: long convertible, short stock. Merger arbitrage: long target.
  29. Independent valuer: not an associate, 3 years' experience, IBBI + ICAI/ICSI/ICMAI/CFA or rating-agency group.
  30. Cat III has no pass-through; F&O gains = business income at MMR.
  31. Listed equity: STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5% above Rs 1.25 lakh; surcharge capped at 15%; cess 4%.
  32. Determinate irrevocable trust: business income at MMR, other income pass-through. Indeterminate: MMR on all.
  33. AIF TDS on distributions 10% (20% if no PAN). Company TDS on dividends 10%.
  34. IFSC: GST exempt, STT exempt, 10-of-15-year tax holiday, MAT 9%, unit holders exempt.
  35. Four pillars of distribution: marketing, sales, investor support, relationship management.
  36. No assured returns, no pass-backs, no soft-dollar deals, disclose commissions.
  37. Key-man insurance: death, incapacity, kidnap, disability. D&O excludes fraud.
  38. PMLA: cash above Rs 10 lakh; wires above Rs 5 lakh; UBO 25% (company/trust), 15% (partnership).
